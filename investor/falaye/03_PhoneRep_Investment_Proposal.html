PhoneRep
A representative in a market that has none.

A two-sided marketplace for phone repair in Lagos: verified technicians, transparent quotes, and payment held in escrow until the customer confirms the phone works. The platform takes 15% of each completed repair and never employs a technician or opens a shop of its own.

The problem

Fixing a phone in Lagos is a trust exercise with no referee. The customer walks into a cluster of shops, is quoted a price that changes once the device is open, is rarely given a written warranty, and has no way to know whether the screen fitted is the one they paid for. When the phone fails again a fortnight later, the conversation starts from zero and the shop has the phone.

The technician's side is no better. A competent repairer in a market of hundreds competes with people who underquote and substitute parts, spends money on a shop location to be found, and has no portable record of the work they have done well. The good ones are indistinguishable from the bad ones until it is too late.

This is not a demand problem. It is an accountability problem, and accountability is a software problem before it is a repair problem.

The model

  • Aggregator, not repairer. No in-house technicians, no physical hub at launch. PhoneRep is the intermediary: it vets the supply, holds the money, and stands behind the job.
  • Escrow by default. The customer pays into the platform. The technician is paid after the customer confirms the repair. If the job fails, the platform mediates before anything is released.
  • Upfront quotes. The customer describes the device and the fault; technicians return comparable quotes. The accepted quote is the price, not a starting position.
  • Three-tier vetting. A digital application filter, a technical evaluation with an engineering advisor, and a signed service-level agreement with cancellation penalties. No walk-ins.
  • A track record that travels. Ratings and completed-job counts belong to the technician, not to a shop front — which is the incentive that keeps quality up.
  • A deliberate disclaimer. PhoneRep is an aggregator. It mediates and it guarantees process, not the physical device forever. That distinction is written into the terms before the first repair.

Fixed cost covers a small core team, baseline customer acquisition, hosting and transactional messaging. Sensitivity: if costs run 20% higher than planned, break-even moves to about 560 repairs a month; if the average ticket falls to ₦15,000, it moves to roughly 620. Both are survivable at the volumes this plan targets, but neither is comfortable — which is why the pilot is measured on completed repairs rather than sign-ups.

Why a 15% take rate holds. The technician gives up 15% and receives qualified demand, a booking and invoicing system, a verified digital storefront and guaranteed payout. That is cheaper than a shop front in Computer Village and it is priced against the alternative, not against nothing.

Risks

RiskWhy it is realMitigation
Supply qualityThe platform's reputation is the technicians'. One botched board repair is a public story.Three-tier vetting, a technical evaluation before onboarding, a warranty reserve funded from the take rate, and removal after two upheld disputes.
Escrow fraud and disputesBoth sides can game a held payment: a customer who will not confirm, a technician who will not return a device.Time-bound auto-release with an inspection window, photo evidence at handover, and a named mediator with written rules in the SLA.
Counterfeit partsCheap screens are the most common way a repair fails.Parts declared in the quote, supplier verification for listed vendors, and a customer-visible parts tier.
Cold-start liquidityA marketplace with no technicians cannot serve customers, and with no customers cannot keep technicians.Launch one corridor at a time with 20–30 technicians, seed demand through referrals, and measure completed repairs rather than sign-ups.
Regulatory and paymentsHolding customer money invites scrutiny, and payment providers can restrict an account mid-pilot.Use a licensed provider's escrow or split-payment product rather than holding balances directly; legal review of the terms before launch.
Key personThis is currently one founder and a document.Operations lead funded in tranche one; engineering advisor named before build starts; runbooks for vetting and mediation.
The one-sentence version. PhoneRep is a documented plan with defensible unit economics and no code yet — so the first ₦4.5m buys a working pilot, and the remaining ₦7.5m is only spent if a hundred real repairs say the model works.
