The first question a careful investor asks about a pre-revenue product is not “what does it do?” It is “why is it worth that?” This page answers with arithmetic and with terms, not with adjectives. Every figure below either comes from Document 02 or is simple multiplication on top of it.

The ask, stated plainly

WorldWorkHub is offered at a ₦120m pre-money valuation. ₦30m buys 20% of the company; the post-money value is ₦150m. That is the whole of the number. There is no option pool shuffle, no convertible note, no discount, and no liquidation preference hidden underneath it. A ticket of ₦3.5m buys 2.8%, and every rung up to ₦30m buys a proportionally larger share at the same price.

Because the product is pre-revenue, the valuation cannot be defended by a multiple on today’s earnings. It has to be defended three other ways: by what has already been built, by what the money is contractually required to produce, and by what happens to the price if it does not.

1 · What the price is made of

Four components. Each one can be checked by you, rather than taken on trust.

ComponentWhat already exists todayHow you check it
Built asset A Manifest V3 Chrome extension at v1.1.0, published on the Chrome Web Store, with a running Express/Mongoose API on Render and a cleaned dataset of roughly 20,000 records after the v1.1.0 de-duplication. Install the extension from the store listing. Hit the public API endpoint. Both are live now, not on a slide.

Twelve months of runway The allocation in Document 02: ₦7m product and engineering, ₦8m data and verification, ₦6m go-to-market, ₦4m infrastructure and legal, ₦5m buffer. That is the full ₦30m, spent on named things. Compare it line by line against Document 02 §Use of funds. Nothing in it is salaries for a team that does not exist.
The option being bought The company-verification database and credibility score. That is the asset that is expensive to copy and that no competitor has built for this market. It does not exist yet. This is the part of the price that is a bet, and I will not pretend otherwise.
The honest split: three of those four components are things you can inspect today. The fourth is the reason the valuation is a pre-revenue valuation rather than a book-value one. If you only believe in the first three, the right instrument for you is the revenue-share facility in Document 01 §Two ways to invest — not equity.

2 · The reverse test — what ₦30m has to produce

Turn the valuation around. If ₦120m pre-money is the price, then the company has to be worth ₦120m at some point for the investment to make sense. Here is what that requires at three different revenue multiples. These are arithmetic, not forecasts.

Exit or next-round multipleAnnual revenue requiredMonthly revenue requiredWhat that looks like
3× revenue₦40.0m₦3.33mThe high end. Needs a paid tier converting well plus sponsored listings.
5× revenue₦24.0m₦2.00mThe middle case this pack is priced against.
8× revenue₦15.0m₦1.25mConservative. Reachable on subscriptions alone if the base is large enough.

The monthly figure is the only number that matters operationally. It converts into users at whatever price the premium tier ends up at — and I am deliberately not inventing that price here, because it has not been tested. So here is the same requirement as a division you can do yourself:

Paying users needed for ₦2.0m a month
at ₦1,000 a month → 2,000 paying users  ·  at ₦2,000 a month → 1,000 paying users  ·  at ₦5,000 a month → 400 paying users

That is the bet in one line: 400 to 2,000 people paying for a job search they currently do badly by hand. If you think that number is unreachable, the valuation is wrong and you should say so. If you think it is reachable but not certain, then the terms below are the answer, because they make the price move with the evidence.

3 · A ratchet instead of an argument

I would rather adjust the price than win the argument. The proposal is that the ₦120m pre-money stands only if the milestone set in Document 02 is delivered. If it is not, the price re-sets downward and your shares are issued at the lower price — you get more of the company for the same ₦30m.

GateWhat has to be trueIf it is not true
Month 4v1.1.0 API contract live on the deployed backend; install and uninstall events measurable for the first time.Release of tranche two is held until it is. This is a payment gate, not a price change.
Month 8Company-verification database in production with a published coverage count; premium checkout taking real payments.Pre-money re-sets to ₦90m. Your ₦30m then buys 25% instead of 20%.
Month 12PWA shipped and a measured baseline for installs, activation and paying users — the three numbers this pack currently marks “not yet measured”.Pre-money re-sets to ₦80m. Your ₦30m then buys 27.3% instead of 20%.

The ratchet figures are proposed terms for negotiation, not measurements. They are set so that a missed milestone costs me dilution rather than costing you cash.

4 · What you are protected by, separately from the price

ProtectionHow it works
Tranche gatesMoney is released against milestones, not handed over in one movement. You always know what the next release is buying.
Buyback at 2×For 24 months you can require the company to buy your position back at twice what you paid. It caps the upside of that route, but it removes the “my money is trapped in a small company” problem entirely.
Revenue share instead of equityIf you do not want paper in a pre-revenue company at all, ₦30m can go in as a revenue-share facility returning capital at 1.4×–1.6× over a capped term. Same money, no valuation argument.
Pro-rata, tag-along, ROFRYou keep your percentage in later rounds, you can exit alongside me, and the shares cannot be sold to a stranger without you having the first right to buy them.
Monthly one-page reportCash in, cash out, installs, paying users, what shipped, what slipped. One page, by the fifth working day, every month.

5 · What would make this valuation wrong

If I am going to ask you to price a pre-revenue company, I should be the one to name the ways I could be wrong.

  • If Chrome Web Store distribution stalls. The extension lives or dies on a marketplace I do not control. A policy change or a delisting is a single point of failure, and the PWA is the mitigation, not a guarantee.
  • If the premium tier does not convert. Freemium conversion is the assumption with the least evidence behind it in this whole pack. It is measurable within one quarter of the checkout going live, which is why that milestone is month 8 and not month 12.
  • If the verification database costs more than ₦8m. It is the largest single line in the allocation and the one I have the least build experience with. If it overruns, the buffer absorbs one overrun, not two.
  • If the market for visa-sponsored listings shrinks. The product is pointed at a policy-sensitive demand. That is a real exposure and no amount of product quality fixes it.
What I am asking you to conclude. Not that ₦120m is certainly right. That it is priced to be corrected: gates on the money, a ratchet on the price, a buyback on the exit, and a revenue-share alternative if you would rather not hold equity at all. You are not being asked to believe a number. You are being asked to accept a number that moves if I fail to earn it.
